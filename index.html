Read this before you copy anything

  • A leaderboard selects for luck as much as skill. Measured live: of 138 traders across the sports leaderboards, only 45 appear in more than one time window and just 2 appear in all four. Traders here are scored on persistence and return-per-dollar, not on one big number.
  • You are always late. Every row shows what they paid against what you'd pay now. The scorecard turns that into a measured number instead of a warning.
  • Agreement is rarer than it looks. Across 45 tracked traders the most any single bet drew was 3 of them. Rows marked split have traders on both sides — that's disagreement, not consensus.
  • You can't see the other side of the book. Size is visible; bankroll, hedges elsewhere and the reason for the trade are not.
  • Research tool over public data. Not advice. These positions can lose their full value.

The traders

Score = 0.45 × persistence + 0.35 × return-on-volume + 0.20 × scale.

Sources. Polymarket Data API (/v1/leaderboard, /activity, /positions) and Gamma API (/markets); ESPN public scoreboards. All public, read-only and unauthenticated — no key, no wallet, no account. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is sent to any server of mine, because there isn't one.

Method. Traders come from four leaderboard windows rather than today's alone, filtered by volume and scored on persistence, return-per-dollar and scale. Their fills from the selected window are grouped by market and outcome. Settled positions (redeemable) are excluded. Sport classification uses Polymarket's sportsMarketType and gameStartTime, with a slug and keyword fallback. Gamma hides closed markets unless asked, so metadata is fetched in two passes — without that, every finished game loses its price and its classification.

Where the scorecard lives. In this browser's local storage only. It is per-browser and per-device — your phone keeps a separate record — and clearing site data erases it. Export a copy from Settings. Kalshi is missing here because it refuses cross-origin browser requests; the Node edition still cross-checks it.
